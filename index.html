Entering clues

From a screenshot: Paste screenshot reads one you’ve copied, and Import file opens a saved one; on a computer, Ctrl/⌘+V also works. Check the letters, fix any that were misread, then choose Use these clues. If the puzzle was solved, the all-green row is left out, so the clues are as they stood just before the answer.

Random makes up a puzzle to practice on: one or two guesses against a past Wordle answer, which stays hidden. It’s chosen so that from a few to a few dozen arrangements fit a word.

‹ Guess 2/3 › appears after a screenshot or Random: the clues as they stood after each guess, here after the second of three. ‹ and › step back and forth, say to see how much one guess narrowed things down. Editing a clue by hand ends the stepping and keeps the clues shown.

By hand:

Green
The letter in each slot you’ve solved. Typing moves to the next slot; space skips one.
Yellow
Under each slot, every letter that turned yellow there, across all your guesses.
Gray
Letters that aren’t in the word. If a letter turned gray in one place but green or yellow in another, enter it here too: the word then has no more copies of it than your green and yellow ones.
Tiles
The yellow letters still to place, one tile each; a letter that’s already green starts with none. If a single guess showed a letter twice in green or yellow, tap it here to add a tile.

Clear starts over. Once you’ve entered a clue, the link icon copies a link to these clues, to share them or to open them on another device. Your clues also come back if you reopen the app the same day.

Arrangements

Every way your yellow tiles can fill the open slots without going where they were yellow. A · marks a slot whose letter you don’t know yet.

Tap an arrangement to put it on the board. Hold it (or right-click it) to see the dictionary words that match it; since that can give the answer away, you’re asked first, once per tab.

A line such as “E must go in slot 4” means every arrangement agrees. Longer lists are sorted into groups by where one letter goes; tap a group heading to pin that letter there. Lists of more than 24 stay folded until you narrow them with pins or choose Show all.

An arrangement is dimmed and struck through when no word in the word list fits it and your clues. When some don’t fit, Dim, Hide and Show choose how they appear.

Put grid first moves the grid of where each letter can go above the list.

Where each letter can go

One row per yellow letter, one column per slot. Each number counts the arrangements that put that letter in that slot. The Unknown row counts slots whose letter you don’t know yet; such a slot may hold another copy of a known letter.

Tap a number to pin the letter there: the list keeps only the arrangements that agree, and the counts update. Tap it again, or choose Clear pins, to undo.

Tap a letter to sort the list by where it goes; tap it again to go back.

More analysis

These figures come from Wordle’s answer list: the original answers plus every word NYT has used. They count words, not letters, and ignore positions.

Vowel odds: of the answers that contain every vowel marked yellow and none marked gray, how many contain each unknown vowel, and how many contain none of them. The vowels start from your clues; tap one to change it, and Reset to clues undoes that.

All clues also uses your consonants: green or yellow ones must be in the word, gray ones must not. It is closer to a solver, so it stays off until you choose it.

Every case of one yellow and one gray vowel: the same odds for each such pair, for reference. On a computer, hover over a cell to see how many answers it covers.

Letter ranking: the share of answers containing each letter, which is the order the Rank view uses.

Letters left

The letters that could still be in the word. Green and yellow keys are from your clues. Gray letters can’t be tapped: Keyboard and Rank keep them in place, and V/C leaves them out.

Tap a letter to try it in the first blank board slot that allows it. On the board, drag a tile to move or swap it, and hold a tried letter to remove it. Green tiles stay fixed.

Fill the board to see whether the word is in the word list and whether it has been a Wordle answer.

Keyboard
Wordle’s keyboard layout.
V/C
Vowels, then Y (in five-letter words it mostly acts as one), then consonants.
Rank
Letters by how many Wordle answers contain them, most common first. The order doesn’t change with your clues, so it’s the same every game. On a computer, hover over a letter to see its share. The More Analysis section has the full table.
