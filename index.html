Entering clues

The card first offers four ways to give your clues; once they are in, it shows a summary of them and the board and the arrangements appear below.

From a screenshot: Paste screenshot reads one you’ve copied, and Choose image opens a saved one; on a computer, Ctrl/⌘+V also works. The guesses go straight to the summary. If a letter was hard to read, the guesses open in the editor instead with that letter outlined in red: if it is wrong, hold it and type the right one, then choose Use these clues. If the puzzle was solved, the all-green row is left out, so the clues are as they stood just before the answer.

Practice puzzle makes up a puzzle to practice on: one or two guesses against a past Wordle answer, which stays hidden. It’s chosen so that from a few to a few dozen arrangements fit a word. A line under the heading says it is a practice puzzle, and New puzzle, in place of Edit clues, draws another.

Type clues opens rows of tiles like Wordle’s. Type each guess you played; a full row opens the next, up to six. Then tap a letter to give it the color Wordle did: once for yellow, twice for green, a third time for gray again. To fix one letter, hold it (or right-click it): it is cleared, the rest of the guess stays, and the next letter you type goes there, in the color the slot had. Backspace takes back the letter before the marked tile, and × empties a guess so you can retype it in the same place. Use these clues takes the guesses once each started one is finished and none contradicts another; if they do, a line under the rows says how. Cancel leaves the clues as they were, and so does your phone’s or browser’s Back.

Double letters need no extra step: two yellow copies of a letter in one guess are two tiles on the board, and a gray copy beside a green or yellow one says the word has no more of that letter.

Guesses lists the words you played. Tap one to see the clues as they stood after it, say to see how much a guess narrowed things down: the later guesses go faint and a line says which guess is shown. Tap the last one to count them all again.

In is a stack with a column per slot, each showing its green letter or else the letters that were yellow there, with any letter the word has twice beside it. Out is the letters that aren’t in the word. Edit clues opens the guesses you typed again, to change or add to them. Clues read straight from a screenshot have no Edit clues: start over and paste the newer screenshot. Nor do clues from a link that carries no guesses. Start over clears everything, board included, and goes back to the four ways in; Undo there puts it all back, until you use other clues or leave the page. Once you’ve entered a clue, the link icon copies a link to these clues, to share them or to open them on another device. Your clues also come back if you reopen the app the same day. Your guesses are kept and shared with them, along with which one is shown.

Legal arrangements

Every way your yellow tiles can fill the open slots without going where they were yellow. A · marks a slot whose letter you don’t know yet. The one on the board is outlined, and the ‹ › arrows under the board cycle through the list in this order.

Tap an arrangement to put it on the board. Hold it (or right-click it) to see the dictionary words that match it; since that can give the answer away, you’re asked first, once per tab.

A line such as “E must go in slot 4” means every arrangement agrees. Longer lists are sorted into groups by where one letter goes; tap a group heading to pin that letter there. Lists of more than 24 stay folded until you narrow them with pins or choose Show all.

Arrangements that no word in the word list fits, given your clues, are left out by default. When there are some, Dim, Hide and Show choose whether they are shown dimmed and struck through, left out, or shown like the rest.

Put grid first, at the bottom of the panel, moves the grid of where each letter can go above the list.

Where each letter can go

One row per yellow letter, one column per slot. Each number counts the arrangements that put that letter in that slot. The Unknown row counts slots whose letter you don’t know yet; such a slot may hold another copy of a known letter.

Tap a number to pin the letter there: the list keeps only the arrangements that agree, and the counts update. Tap it again, or choose Clear pins, to undo.

Tap a letter to sort the list by where it goes; tap it again to go back.

Reference tables

Two tables from Wordle’s answer list: the original answers plus every word NYT has used. They count words, not letters, ignore positions, and don’t change with your clues.

Letters in Wordle answers: the share of answers containing each letter, most common first. The Rank view of the letters under the board starts from this order and narrows it to the answers that fit your clues.

Every case of one yellow and one gray vowel: for each such pair, how likely each other vowel is, and the chance of none; the case your clues match is outlined. On a computer, hover over a cell to see how many answers it covers.

The board and the letters

The board shows one legal arrangement of your yellow tiles, with green letters fixed. Drag a yellow tile to another slot to move it, or onto another yellow tile to swap them. It won’t go to a slot where it was yellow, marked by the yellow letters above: the slot shows red as you drag over it and the tile stays where it was. The yellow letters over a slot are the ones that were yellow there: the word has them, but not in that slot. Above the board, ‹ Arrangement 3 of 12 › says which of the listed arrangements is showing, and ‹ › cycle through them in the list’s order, round and round. Putting another arrangement on the board, from there or from the list, clears the letters you had tried. Dragging a tile keeps them, except one it is dropped on, which it replaces.

Available letters, under the board, are the ones that could still be in the word; green and yellow keys are from your clues. Tap a letter to try it on the board: in the empty slot you chose (tap one first), or else in the first blank slot that allows it. A tried letter can be dragged too: onto an empty slot to move it, onto another tried letter to take its place, or onto a yellow tile to trade places with it. Its × removes it (on a touch screen, holding it works as well).

Gray letters don’t go on the board: Keyboard keeps them in place, dimmed, and V/C and Rank leave them out.

Hold a letter (or right-click it) to see what share of the answers that still fit your clues contain it. With a mouse, hovering over it says the same.

Fill the board to see whether the word is in the word list and whether it has been a Wordle answer.

Keyboard
Wordle’s keyboard layout.
V/C
Vowels, then Y (in five-letter words it mostly acts as one), then consonants.
Rank
Letters by how many of the answers that fit all your clues contain them, most common first: green and yellow letters lead, gray ones are left out, and a line under the keys says how many answers that is. When no answer fits your clues, it is the order over all answers, the table under Reference tables.
